# Three-Hour Ethics Class Presentation Plan
## Topic: Social Media Manipulation vs. Persuasion (and AI)

## Slide 1 — Title & Framing
- **Course:** Ethics Seminar
- **Session:** Social Media Manipulation vs. Persuasion
- **Duration:** 3 hours
- **Goal:** Distinguish ethical persuasion from unethical manipulation in social media and AI-mediated systems.

## Slide 2 — Learning Objectives
By the end of this class, students should be able to:
1. Define persuasion, manipulation, influence, and coercion.
2. Identify manipulative design patterns in social media products.
3. Evaluate ethical boundaries using multiple frameworks.
4. Analyze how AI intensifies persuasion/manipulation dynamics.
5. Propose policy, design, and personal strategies for mitigation.

## Slide 3 — Why This Matters
- Social media shapes beliefs, identities, and democratic outcomes.
- Attention is monetized; behavior is engineered at scale.
- AI now enables hyper-targeted, personalized influence.
- Harms affect mental health, civic trust, and vulnerable populations.

## Slide 4 — Core Definitions
- **Persuasion:** Transparent attempt to change beliefs/behavior while preserving autonomy.
- **Manipulation:** Hidden, deceptive, or exploitative influence that undermines autonomy.
- **Nudge:** Choice architecture steering behavior without forbidding options.
- **Dark pattern:** Interface design that benefits platform interests over user welfare.

## Slide 5 — Ethical Boundary Test
Ask:
- Is the intent disclosed?
- Can users understand and resist the influence?
- Is vulnerability being exploited?
- Are outcomes primarily for user good or platform profit/power?
- Is informed consent meaningful?

## Slide 6 — Three-Hour Session Agenda
### Hour 1: Concepts & Foundations (0:00–1:00)
- Mini-lecture on definitions and history of propaganda/advertising.
- Comparative examples: persuasive public health campaign vs manipulative clickbait funnel.
- Quick poll + discussion.

### Hour 2: Case Analysis (1:00–2:00)
- Group breakout on real-world cases.
- Ethical analysis using frameworks.
- Report-outs and instructor synthesis.

### Hour 3: AI Expansion + Solutions Lab (2:00–3:00)
- AI-generated influence risks.
- Governance options and design interventions.
- Team activity: draft an “Ethical Influence Charter.”

## Slide 7 — Hour 1 Activity: Spot the Difference
Compare two campaigns:
1. Smoking cessation campaign using emotional storytelling.
2. Platform growth campaign using FOMO and misleading urgency.

Prompt:
- Which is persuasion vs manipulation?
- Where is user autonomy respected or violated?
- What evidence supports your judgment?

## Slide 8 — Mechanisms of Social Media Influence
- Social proof (likes, shares, followers)
- Scarcity and urgency cues
- Repetition and mere-exposure effects
- Emotional arousal and outrage amplification
- Parasocial trust in creators/influencers

## Slide 9 — Manipulative Product Tactics (Dark Patterns)
- Infinite scroll and variable reward loops
- Hard-to-find privacy settings
- Default opt-ins for tracking
- Misleading consent banners
- Frictionless sharing, high-friction account deletion

## Slide 10 — Psychological Vulnerabilities
- Cognitive overload and heuristic thinking
- Confirmation bias and motivated reasoning
- Loneliness, anxiety, and social belonging needs
- Adolescent developmental sensitivity
- Crisis moments (financial, health, political uncertainty)

## Slide 11 — Economic Incentives
- Ad-based business models optimize engagement, not truth/well-being.
- Algorithmic ranking favors emotionally provocative content.
- Creator monetization pressures sensational content.
- Data extraction enables precise behavioral prediction and steering.

## Slide 12 — Case Set A: Political Manipulation
- Microtargeted political ads with opaque audiences
- Bot/amplification networks
- Disinformation cascades during elections

Discussion:
- What is legal vs ethical?
- Who should be accountable (platforms, campaigns, regulators, users)?

## Slide 13 — Case Set B: Health & Wellness Misinfo
- Anti-vaccine narratives, miracle cures, fitness scams
- Influencer authority without expertise
- Engagement algorithms rewarding controversy

Discussion:
- Distinguish harmful persuasion from protected speech.
- What interventions minimize harm while preserving rights?

## Slide 14 — Case Set C: Youth & Body Image
- Beauty filters, comparison loops, toxic challenges
- “Thinspiration” and algorithmic reinforcement
- Sponsored content disguised as authenticity

Discussion:
- Where is the line between aspiration and exploitation?
- What protections are owed to minors?

## Slide 15 — Ethical Frameworks for Analysis
Use multiple lenses:
- **Utilitarianism:** net harms/benefits at population scale
- **Deontology:** duties, rights, informed consent, truthfulness
- **Virtue ethics:** character of designers/leaders and civic virtues
- **Care ethics:** impacts on vulnerable groups and relationships
- **Justice/fairness:** who bears risk vs who captures value

## Slide 16 — Structured Case Worksheet
For each case, teams answer:
1. Stakeholders and power asymmetries
2. Intended and foreseeable effects
3. Consent and transparency quality
4. Alternatives with less autonomy violation
5. Recommended action and justification

## Slide 17 — Transition to AI: What Changes?
AI increases scale, speed, and precision of influence:
- Real-time personalization and adaptive messaging
- Synthetic media generation at low cost
- Automated experimentation across user segments
- “Always-on” conversational persuasion agents

## Slide 18 — AI-Enabled Manipulation Patterns
- Deepfakes and synthetic personas
- Emotion recognition + mood-based targeting
- LLM chatbots steering beliefs over long interactions
- Coordinated inauthentic behavior with AI-generated content
- Astroturfing through AI-assisted sockpuppet networks

## Slide 19 — Persuasion vs Manipulation in AI Systems
Ethical AI persuasion may include:
- Transparent recommender explanations
- User-controlled preference settings
- Benefit-aligned behavioral coaching with consent

Manipulative AI includes:
- Hidden optimization toward engagement/addiction
- Deceptive anthropomorphism (“I care about you” for retention)
- Exploiting sensitive traits inferred from behavior

## Slide 20 — Additional Relevant Social Media Issues
- Privacy erosion and surveillance capitalism
- Algorithmic bias and discriminatory visibility
- Harassment, dogpiling, and safety failures
- Labor exploitation in content moderation
- Platform governance opacity and weak appeals processes

## Slide 21 — AI-Specific Ethical Tensions
- Personalization vs autonomy
- Safety filtering vs censorship concerns
- Explainability vs proprietary models
- Open models vs misuse risks
- Human oversight vs automated enforcement errors

## Slide 22 — Legal & Policy Landscape (High Level)
- Data protection and privacy regulation
- Digital services/transparency obligations
- Political ad disclosure requirements
- Child online safety proposals
- AI governance: risk management and auditing expectations

## Slide 23 — Design Interventions
- Humane defaults (chronological options, notification controls)
- Friction for high-risk sharing (read-before-share prompts)
- Clear ad labels and provenance markers
- Periodic consent renewal for sensitive targeting
- Independent audits for recommender systems

## Slide 24 — Individual & Community Defenses
- Media literacy and lateral reading habits
- “Pause before share” protocols
- Source triangulation and credibility checklists
- Well-being boundaries (time limits, notification hygiene)
- Peer norms for responsible digital behavior

## Slide 25 — In-Class Exercise: Ethical Influence Charter
Teams draft a one-page charter with:
- Non-negotiable principles
- Red lines (forbidden practices)
- Transparency commitments
- Accountability mechanisms
- Incident response plan for harmful influence events

## Slide 26 — Debrief Questions
- Can manipulation ever be justified for social good?
- Who decides acceptable influence boundaries?
- Is informed consent possible in complex algorithmic systems?
- What should be banned vs merely disclosed?

## Slide 26B — Extended Discussion Question Bank (Part 1)
- When does emotional storytelling become emotional exploitation?
- Should platforms be treated like publishers, utilities, or something else?
- Is algorithmic amplification morally equivalent to speech?
- How should we weigh free expression against measurable public harm?
- What obligations do influencers have to verify claims before sharing?
- Can users be said to “consent” to systems they cannot realistically understand?
- Are engagement metrics inherently unethical, or only their misuse?
- Should persuasive design be taught as a required ethics competency in tech programs?
- What is the fairest way to protect children without over-surveilling them?
- Is omission (not showing corrective information) a form of manipulation?

## Slide 26C — Extended Discussion Question Bank (Part 2)
- Should governments set limits on personalization for political content?
- Are anonymous accounts net-positive for safety and speech, or net-negative for abuse?
- How much transparency is enough for recommender systems?
- Do labels like “AI-generated” actually reduce deception in practice?
- Who is most harmed when moderation systems fail—individuals or democracy?
- Should “time spent” ever be a success metric for social products?
- Can a platform be ethical if its business model depends on addictive behaviors?
- Are outrage-driven posts manipulative by default, or context-dependent?
- Should public health misinformation be removed, downranked, or countered with context?
- What role should schools play in building anti-manipulation literacy?

## Slide 26D — Extended Discussion Question Bank (Part 3: AI)
- Is an AI assistant that “mirrors” a user’s beliefs manipulative or supportive?
- Should AI systems be allowed to infer sensitive traits for personalization?
- Are synthetic companions ethical if users become emotionally dependent on them?
- Is persuasive AI in mental health coaching acceptable under strict consent?
- Should AI-generated political persuasion be prohibited during election windows?
- If an AI is non-human, must it be forbidden from simulating empathy?
- Does explainability meaningfully protect users if models remain too complex?
- Should users have a legal right to opt out of algorithmic persuasion entirely?
- Is it ethical for AI agents to negotiate, upsell, or pressure users in commerce?
- What minimum safeguards should be mandatory before deploying conversational AI at scale?

## Slide 26E — Debate Motion Set (Oxford-Style)
Use these as formal class debates with proposition/opposition teams:
- **Resolved:** Most social media platforms are manipulative by design.
- **Resolved:** Microtargeted political advertising should be banned.
- **Resolved:** Algorithmic feeds should default to chronological order.
- **Resolved:** Engagement optimization is incompatible with ethical platform design.
- **Resolved:** Platforms should be legally liable for foreseeable amplification harms.
- **Resolved:** Deepfake tools should require licensing and identity verification.
- **Resolved:** AI chatbots should be barred from giving persuasive political advice.
- **Resolved:** Influencers should face stricter disclosure rules than traditional advertisers.
- **Resolved:** User autonomy is impossible under current recommender architectures.
- **Resolved:** Digital manipulation should be regulated like public health risk.

## Slide 26F — Rapid-Fire Mini Debate Prompts
For 5–8 minute rounds:
- “Nudging for good is still manipulation.”
- “If users can leave, design cannot be coercive.”
- “Transparency disclosures mostly function as legal cover.”
- “Attention extraction is the tobacco model of the digital age.”
- “Platform neutrality is a myth.”
- “AI companions should be age-restricted.”
- “Fact-check labels do more harm than good.”
- “Anonymity should be preserved even if abuse increases.”
- “Banning bad content is less effective than redesigning incentives.”
- “The right to be unpersuaded should be a digital right.”

## Slide 26G — Role-Based Discussion Scenarios
Assign one role per group and debate tradeoffs:
- Platform safety lead balancing growth targets and harm reduction.
- Teen user advocate demanding stronger protections and transparency.
- Civil liberties lawyer focused on overreach and censorship risk.
- Public health official confronting misinformation surges.
- Election integrity researcher analyzing coordinated influence operations.
- Content creator dependent on algorithmic visibility for income.
- AI product manager shipping conversational features under deadline pressure.
- Regulator designing enforceable but innovation-friendly policy.

## Slide 27 — Assessment Options
- Reflection memo: analyze one platform feature
- Group policy brief: reduce manipulation risk in one domain
- Debate: “Resolved—Most recommender systems are manipulative by design.”
- Design critique: redesign a dark pattern into an ethical alternative

## Slide 28 — Suggested Readings
- Shoshana Zuboff, *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism*
- Cailin O’Connor & James Owen Weatherall, *The Misinformation Age*
- Safiya Umoja Noble, *Algorithms of Oppression*
- Center for Humane Technology reports
- Recent AI governance framework summaries (NIST/OECD/UNESCO)

## Slide 29 — Instructor Notes (Facilitation Tips)
- Balance moral clarity with nuance.
- Encourage evidence-based argument, not just opinion.
- Protect room climate in politically sensitive discussions.
- Prioritize voices of students affected by online harms.

## Slide 30 — Closing
- Re-state key distinction: **ethical persuasion respects agency; manipulation subverts it.**
- Emphasize system-level and individual responsibilities.
- Invite students to apply the framework to their own media ecosystems this week.
