# CSCI 421 Final Ethics Paper Outline & Weekly Writing Plan

Use this guide with the **CSCI421_Final_Paper_Guidelines.pdf** requirements. The final paper is **10–15 pages** and should analyze a real or emerging technology through ethical frameworks, then recommend a responsible course of action.

## Core Requirements (from the course guidelines)
Your final paper should include:
1. A **clear ethical dilemma**.
2. Use of **at least two ethical frameworks** (**Rawls is required**).
3. Analysis of **individual and societal impacts**.
4. **Economic value analysis** (costs, benefits, efficiency, long-term consequences).
5. A **defensible conclusion** based on careful reasoning.

---

## Citation Requirements for This Project Plan
- Final paper must include **at least 7 different citations**.
- Each week, write **2–3 pages** and include **1–2 citations**.
- By the final week, confirm all citations are credible and relevant (academic articles, books, reputable reports, major journalism, policy documents, or primary technical documentation).
- Use **Chicago citation style** consistently for in-text notes/footnotes and the final bibliography (follow your instructor's preferred Chicago variant).

## Recommended 5-Week Schedule (2–3 Pages per Week)

## Week 1 (2–3 pages): Topic, Context, and Thesis Direction
### Writing goals
- Introduce the technology issue (example: AI surveillance, data privacy, social media manipulation, algorithmic bias, autonomous weapons, etc.).
- Explain why the issue matters now.
- Define the key stakeholders (users, developers, companies, governments, vulnerable populations).
- Draft a preliminary thesis: what ethical tension are you investigating?

### Include 1–2 citations
- 1 source for background/facts.
- 1 source for recent real-world example/case.

### Deliverable checklist
- [ ] Working title
- [ ] Problem statement (1 paragraph)
- [ ] Early ethical dilemma statement
- [ ] 1–2 cited sources

---

## Week 2 (2–3 pages): Ethical Dilemma + Framework Setup
### Writing goals
- Clearly define the central ethical dilemma in one concise paragraph.
- Present the first ethical framework (e.g., Rawls).
- Present a second framework (e.g., utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, care ethics, rights-based approach).
- Define the framework standards you will use to evaluate the issue.

### Include 1–2 citations
- 1 source on Rawls or ethical theory.
- 1 source on second framework or applied ethics in technology.

### Deliverable checklist
- [ ] Explicit dilemma statement
- [ ] Rawls framework section
- [ ] Second framework section
- [ ] Criteria for comparison
- [ ] 1–2 additional citations

---

## Week 3 (2–3 pages): Application of Frameworks to the Case
### Writing goals
- Apply Rawls directly to your case (fairness, rights, distribution of harms/benefits).
- Apply your second framework to the same case.
- Compare the frameworks: where they agree, where they conflict.
- Address counterarguments or common objections.

### Include 1–2 citations
- 1 source with case-specific evidence (data, harms, outcomes).
- 1 source with policy/legal/industry perspective.

### Deliverable checklist
- [ ] Rawls analysis applied to evidence
- [ ] Second framework applied to evidence
- [ ] Comparison paragraph/table
- [ ] Counterargument + response
- [ ] 1–2 additional citations

---

## Week 4 (2–3 pages): Societal + Individual Impacts and Economic Value
### Writing goals
- Analyze impacts on individuals (privacy, autonomy, safety, dignity, opportunity).
- Analyze broader societal effects (equity, trust, democracy, labor, social cohesion).
- Complete economic value analysis:
  - short-term vs. long-term costs,
  - who receives benefits,
  - who bears risks,
  - whether efficiency gains justify tradeoffs.
- Explain why economic benefit matters ethically but does not automatically justify harm.

### Include 1–2 citations
- 1 source on social/individual impacts.
- 1 source on economic impacts, incentives, or policy costs/benefits.

### Deliverable checklist
- [ ] Individual impact section
- [ ] Societal impact section
- [ ] Economic value section
- [ ] Ethical interpretation of economic findings
- [ ] 1–2 additional citations

---

## Week 5 (2–3 pages): Conclusion, Recommendation, and Final Edit
### Writing goals
- State your final ethical judgment and defend it.
- Propose a responsible course of action (policy, design, governance, education, transparency, accountability, etc.).
- Explain implementation challenges and how to mitigate them.
- Conclude with ethical clarity: what should decision-makers do, and why?

### Include 1–2 citations
- Add any missing supporting sources.
- Ensure total citations are **7+ different sources**.

### Deliverable checklist
- [ ] Defensible conclusion tied to frameworks
- [ ] Actionable recommendation(s)
- [ ] Limitations/future concerns
- [ ] Final works cited / references page
- [ ] Citation count check (7+ unique sources)

---

## Suggested Final Paper Structure (10–15 pages total)
1. **Introduction (1–2 pages)**
   - Hook, context, significance, thesis.
2. **Issue Description and Stakeholders (1–2 pages)**
   - Technology background, actors, stakes.
3. **Ethical Dilemma Statement (1 page)**
   - Precise dilemma and competing values.
4. **Framework 1: Rawls (1–2 pages)**
   - Key principles and relevance.
5. **Framework 2: Chosen Ethical Framework (1–2 pages)**
   - Key principles and relevance.
6. **Comparative Ethical Analysis (2–3 pages)**
   - Apply both frameworks, weigh conflicts.
7. **Societal + Individual Impacts (1–2 pages)**
   - Harm/benefit distribution and equity concerns.
8. **Economic Value Analysis (1–2 pages)**
   - Costs, benefits, tradeoffs, long-term effects.
9. **Conclusion + Responsible Recommendation (1–2 pages)**
   - Final judgment and clear action steps.

## Writing Quality Checklist
- [ ] Topic is specific, not too broad.
- [ ] Ethical dilemma is clearly defined.
- [ ] Rawls is used accurately and meaningfully.
- [ ] Second framework is explained and applied correctly.
- [ ] Claims are evidence-based and cited.
- [ ] At least 7 unique citations are included.
- [ ] Chicago citation style is used consistently throughout the paper and bibliography.
- [ ] Conclusion is clear, practical, and defensible.
- [ ] Paper stays within 10–15 pages (plus references if allowed by instructor).
