# Week Three Discussion Guide: CyberEthics — Privacy (Zuboff Excerpt)

**Session length:** 3 hours

## Session goals
- Connect Zuboff’s ideas to modern data broker practices and online surveillance.
- Explore privacy as a right, commodity, and/or illusion.
- Examine privacy risks in the internet and AI contexts, including extreme scenarios.

## Pre-class materials
- **Reading:** Zuboff excerpt (CyberEthics: Privacy chapter)
- **Watch:** *Last Week Tonight* — “Data Brokers” (John Oliver)
- **Journal prompt:** *Is privacy a right, a commodity, or an illusion?*

---

## Class structure (3 hours)
**0:00–0:15 — Opening check-in**
- Quick write: One line on what “privacy” means today.
- 2–3 volunteers share.

**0:15–0:45 — Shared framing**
- Mini-lecture or recap: Zuboff’s key claims.
- Highlight data broker ecosystem from the video.

**0:45–1:45 — Small-group discussion rounds**
- Divide into groups of 4–6.
- Each group tackles one set of questions (below) and prepares a 3–5 minute report-out.

**1:45–2:00 — Break**

**2:00–2:40 — Whole-class report-outs**
- Each group shares insights and key disagreements.
- Instructor collects themes on board.

**2:40–3:00 — Synthesis & closing**
- Revisit journal prompt and invite revised answers.
- Exit ticket: One policy or personal practice change you’d support.

---

## Small-group discussion questions
### Group Set A — Zuboff + surveillance capitalism
1. What is Zuboff’s core argument about how data is extracted and monetized?
2. Where do you see “behavioral surplus” in everyday apps or services?
3. Is surveillance capitalism inevitable, or are there realistic alternatives?
4. How does consent work (or fail) in these systems?

### Group Set B — Data brokers (John Oliver)
1. What surprised you most about the data broker ecosystem?
2. Should data brokers be legal at all? Why or why not?
3. What does “opt out” actually mean in practice—does it work?
4. Who is most harmed by brokered data, and why?

### Group Set C — Privacy: right, commodity, or illusion?
1. Is privacy primarily a human right, a market good, or an illusion? Explain.
2. Can privacy be negotiated or sold without coercion?
3. What does “informed consent” look like online—if it exists at all?
4. Should certain data be non-sellable (e.g., health, location, biometrics)?

### Group Set D — Internet + AI privacy risks
1. How do AI systems change the privacy equation compared to traditional data collection?
2. What are the privacy risks of training AI on public vs. private data?
3. What protections should apply to AI-generated inferences about people?
4. How should accountability work when AI causes privacy harm?

---

## Whole-class report-out prompts
- Where did your group agree? Where did you disagree?
- What is the most **dangerous** privacy trend you see today?
- What is the most **promising** policy or technology for privacy?
- What should be illegal in a decade that is legal today?

## Discussion topics on privacy in the internet and AI
### Advantages (pro-privacy and pro-data perspectives)
- Personalized services and improved user experiences.
- Fraud detection, security, and safety improvements.
- Public health insights and research benefits.
- Accessibility and adaptive tools.

### Disadvantages (privacy costs and tradeoffs)
- Loss of autonomy and chilling effects on speech.
- Manipulation through behavioral targeting.
- Discrimination and exclusion based on inferred traits.
- Unequal burdens on marginalized communities.

### Potentially really bad options (worst-case outcomes)
- Ubiquitous real-time surveillance paired with AI prediction.
- De facto “social credit” systems without accountability.
- Permanent reputational scoring tied to employment/housing.
- AI-generated dossiers that individuals cannot access or correct.
- Data breaches enabling identity theft at scale.

## Suggested report-out structure (for student groups)
1. **Key idea:** One sentence.
2. **Evidence:** From Zuboff, John Oliver, or personal experience.
3. **Tension:** A disagreement or unresolved question.
4. **Recommendation:** Policy, technology, or personal practice.
