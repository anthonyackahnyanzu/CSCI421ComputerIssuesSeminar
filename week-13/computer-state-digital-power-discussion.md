# Week 13 Class Materials
## Topic: The Computer State and Digital Power

**Syllabus alignment (Week 13):**
- Core theme: **State capacity, public technology, and digital governance**
- Guiding question: **How can governments use technology to improve public services and quality of life before digital power creates preventable harms?**

---

## 1) Short Opening Presentation

### A. Framing the Week
- The “computer state” describes how governments increasingly rely on data systems, platforms, and automation to make decisions, deliver services, and enforce rules.
- Digital power can expand public capacity in areas like health, education, transit, and emergency response.
- But systems that increase efficiency can also centralize control, widen inequality, and weaken democratic accountability if poorly designed.

### B. Key Concepts for Today
1. **Digital state capacity**
   - Using technology to deliver services faster, cheaper, and more accurately.
   - Examples: online benefits portals, digital IDs, interoperable records, smart infrastructure.

2. **Administrative burden**
   - Time, confusion, and paperwork that keep people from receiving services they qualify for.
   - Good civic technology should reduce friction for residents, not add new barriers.

3. **Digital power and legitimacy**
   - Public trust depends on transparency, appeal rights, and human accountability.
   - A technically “working” system can still be ethically unacceptable.

### C. Public-Service Opportunities Before Problems
- **Proactive service delivery:** identify eligible residents for benefits (with consent and safeguards) instead of requiring complex applications.
- **Life-event government:** bundle services around events like birth, job loss, disability, or retirement.
- **Crisis response modernization:** real-time coordination for wildfire, flood, heat, and public health emergencies.
- **Inclusive access design:** multilingual, mobile-first, and low-bandwidth systems for rural and underserved communities.
- **Data-informed infrastructure:** optimize buses, maintenance, and utility repairs to reduce delays and waste.

### D. Ethical Warning
- If governments move too fast without safeguards, digital systems can produce surveillance creep, wrongful denials, opaque scoring, and discrimination at scale.
- The priority is not “digitize everything,” but “digitize responsibly with rights protections from day one.”

## 2) Group Discussion Activity (3 Groups)

Break into **three groups**. Each group will:
1. Discuss assigned questions.
2. Build one practical proposal.
3. Identify risks and safeguards *before* deployment.

### Group 1 — Digital Public Services and Quality of Life
**Focus:** How governments can improve everyday services using technology.

**Discussion questions:**
1. Which public service in your city/state is most frustrating today, and how could digital redesign improve it?
2. What “life-event” service bundle (birth, unemployment, disability, housing instability, elder care) would make the biggest difference?
3. How can governments design systems for people with low digital literacy, disabilities, or limited internet access?
4. Should default enrollment in certain programs be expanded if eligibility is already known?
5. What metrics should define success: speed, access rate, user satisfaction, equity outcomes, or all of the above?

**Deliverable:**
- A **“Top 3 Civic Tech Upgrades”** brief with:
  - 3 concrete service improvements,
  - expected quality-of-life benefit for each,
  - one implementation challenge per proposal.

---

### Group 2 — Digital Identity, Data Sharing, and Trust
**Focus:** Coordinating agencies to improve services without building a surveillance state.

**Discussion questions:**
1. Should governments create a unified digital identity system? Why or why not?
2. Which kinds of inter-agency data sharing are ethically justified to reduce administrative burden?
3. Where should strict “no-share” boundaries exist (health data, immigration status, juvenile records, etc.)?
4. How should consent work when people need services quickly?
5. What independent oversight mechanisms would make residents trust these systems?

**Deliverable:**
- A **“Trust-by-Design Framework”** including:
  - 3 approved data-sharing uses,
  - 3 prohibited uses,
  - 2 accountability tools (audit logs, ombuds office, public reporting, etc.).

---

### Group 3 — Algorithmic Decisions in Government
**Focus:** Using automation for triage and resource allocation before harms become entrenched.

**Discussion questions:**
1. Which government decisions should **never** be fully automated (benefits denial, sentencing, deportation, child welfare, etc.)?
2. When can algorithmic triage be useful (case prioritization, inspection scheduling, fraud detection)?
3. What rights should residents have when affected by automated decisions (notice, explanation, appeal, human review)?
4. How can governments prevent bias from historical data from being repeated at larger scale?
5. What should happen if an algorithm appears accurate overall but harms one subgroup disproportionately?

**Deliverable:**
- An **“Algorithmic Use Policy”** with:
  - 3 permitted use cases,
  - 3 prohibited/high-risk uses,
  - mandatory procedural protections for affected residents.

---

## 3) Whole-Class Synthesis Prompts
- Which proposal would most quickly improve people’s lives in the next 12 months?
- Where should governments prioritize innovation first: benefits, health, housing, transit, or education?
- What trade-offs are acceptable for efficiency, and which rights are non-negotiable?
- What oversight mechanism should be mandatory for every high-impact public algorithm?

## 4) Policy Design Challenge (Optional if time allows)
Draft a one-page policy memo titled:
**“Digital Government First Principles for Public Good.”**

Include:
1. One service area to modernize immediately.
2. One measurable equity goal.
3. One legal safeguard.
4. One transparency requirement.
5. One public accountability process.

---

## 5) Exit Ticket
In 4–6 sentences, respond to both:
1. What is one way government technology could immediately improve daily life in your community?
2. What is one risk that must be addressed *before* launching that system?
